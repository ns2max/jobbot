FIELD 1 — "Can you share examples of past work or relevant links?"
----------------------------------------------------------------

Papers

- Real-Time Audio Pattern Detection for Smart Musical Instruments. Journal of
  the Audio Engineering Society 74(3), 2026. https://doi.org/10.17743/jaes.2022.0250
  My core PhD result: a real-time detector on noisy streaming audio that runs
  inference in 14 ms on a Raspberry Pi 4 (F1 0.76, 74x faster than the
  1038 ms baseline it replaced). The paper is as much about the benchmark
  protocol and ablations used to show the gain was real as about the model.

- User-Centered Evaluation of Smart Musical Instruments with Embedded Real-Time
  Pattern Detection. IEEE I3DA 2025. https://doi.org/10.1109/i3da65421.2025.11202088
  A study combining quantitative metrics with structured interviews to check
  whether the system's improvements held up with real users, not just on a
  test set.

- On Musical Onset Detection via the S-Transform. 52nd Asilomar Conference on
  Signals, Systems and Computers, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACSSC.2018.8645367
  Signal-processing work: using the Stockwell transform to detect events in
  noisy audio where a fixed time-frequency resolution fails.

Full list and citation metrics: https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=pdintvAAAAAJ

FIELD 2 — "What do you think we should know about you, and why are
you excited to join Eli?"
----------------------------------------------------------------

The through-line of my work is turning imperfect, noisy real-world sensor data
into models that hold up. In my PhD I owned the full pipeline for a real-time
audio system, from the DSP front end through training and evaluation to
deployment on a Raspberry Pi, and most of the effort went into the part your
posting names directly: designing experiments and frozen benchmarks that could
tell a genuine improvement apart from one that only moved a metric, and keeping
an honest record of what did not work. Before that I spent five and a half
years at MAS Holdings shipping computer-vision and sensor systems onto factory
floors, where a model's output had to survive real conditions and real
operators, and one deployment was a COVID-19 remote-vitals device used across
hospital wards. That is the closest I have come to health signals. Assay
chemistry and hormone monitoring would be new to me, but learning an applied
field cold is a pattern in my record, from apparel manufacturing to financial
forensics.

I am excited about Eli because it puts machine learning to work on a problem
that matters to people's everyday health, in an early team where the work has
direct impact. Making a difficult measurement reliable enough to trust at home
is exactly the kind of applied problem I want to spend my time on, and a small
team with real ownership is where I have always done my best work.
