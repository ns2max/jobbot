I am applying for the Machine Learning Researcher role at RBC Borealis. My work has consistently been about advancing machine learning research and then translating it into production. My time-series and real-time pattern-detection research is one example: the published detector in my postdoc reaches F1 0.76 in 14 ms on a Raspberry Pi 4, 74 times faster than the dynamic time warping baseline it replaced (JAES 2026), and that result came out of frozen-protocol benchmarks and ablations as much as the model itself. It did not stop at a paper; it shipped as a deployed system with monitoring.

The role asks for a researcher to advance ML across time series and generative AI, work with large datasets, collaborate with engineering to translate research into production-grade solutions, and publish. Time-series and sequence modelling (RNN/LSTM/GRU, benchmark and evaluation design) is my core, and I have built VAE- and diffusion-based synthetic-data prototypes for signals and time series to handle limited-label regimes. I work routinely with large datasets: four open-access corpora I designed and released (9,800+ recordings, 70+ participants), and high-frequency IoT ETL across factories in South Asia, North America and Africa at MAS Holdings. Translating research to production is the through-line of both my postdoc, where I owned the science and the pipeline for a novel real-time system, and my MAS years, where research prototypes reached the factory floor with CI/CD and code review. I have published 10+ peer-reviewed papers, review for IEEE Access and the Journal of the National Science Foundation of Sri Lanka, and sit on the programme committees of IEEE IS\textsuperscript{2} and IEEE I3DA.

RBC Borealis is a research institute inside RBC with rich datasets, computational resources and explicit support for publication and conferences, working across generative AI, NLP and time-series analysis for banking. The connection I bring is direct on two of those: my time-series research, and my work at Forestpin building financial-forensics anomaly detection, compliance monitoring and production scoring services on enterprise financial data. I want to be honest about the rest. NLP is not a track I have, and my publication venues (JAES, IEEE IS\textsuperscript{2}, DAFx, Asilomar) are strong in audio and signal processing but not the NeurIPS or ICML tier. What stands alongside them is the open datasets, the benchmark methodology, and the journal and programme-committee review service.

I am a Canadian permanent resident, eligible to work without sponsorship, and available immediately since my postdoctoral contract ended in December 2025. I am based in Toronto and happy to relocate within Canada for an RBC Borealis office, Calgary or another. My publications, datasets and portfolio are at \href{\clSite}{nishal.xyz}.
