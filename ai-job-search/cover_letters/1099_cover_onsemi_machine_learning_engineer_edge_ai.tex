\clAddressee

I am applying for the Machine Learning Engineer, Edge AI role at onsemi. I am a Machine Learning Research Engineer with a PhD from the University of Trento and more than ten years across industry and academia, and my specialty is exactly the boundary this role sits on: getting models to run accurately inside hard latency, compute, power and memory budgets on constrained hardware. My published work runs real-time audio pattern detection in 14 ms on a Raspberry Pi 4 at 31.4 percent CPU, F1 0.76, which is 74 times faster than the dynamic time warping baseline it replaced (JAES 2026).

The posting asks for ML across time-series, audio, computer vision and ultrasonic domains, and for someone who can move a model from framework to constrained target while reasoning about the tradeoffs. That has been my work for a decade. At the University of Trento I owned the full pipeline for streaming audio and sensor data, from DSP and feature engineering through training to deployment on Raspberry Pi with monitoring, and I designed the baseline and ablation suites that quantified how architecture, feature rate and pattern-set size trade against latency and CPU. At MAS Holdings I shipped C++ and Python inference engines on embedded and IoT hardware at manufacturing scale, including computer-vision inspection that cut label QC time by 99.5 percent, and I did that in close co-design with the hardware, firmware and PLC teams. My deep learning work has been primarily in TensorFlow with PyTorch and ONNX alongside it; accelerator kernel programming is where I would ramp fastest, building on the ARM profiling and latency benchmarking behind my open-source C++17 library Nebula. I prototype quickly, including with agentic coding tools such as Claude Code.

What draws me to onsemi specifically is the Treo platform direction: moving analog, sensing and processing into a single device so that intelligence lives where the signal is captured rather than downstream. That is the same problem I have chased from the other side, making the model small and fast enough to belong on the sensor. In the first months I would contribute the model-side rigour that accelerator bring-up needs: principled architecture selection, feature-rate tuning, and a benchmark discipline that makes the accuracy, latency and power tradeoffs measurable rather than guessed.

I am a Canadian permanent resident based in Toronto, eligible to work without sponsorship, available immediately, and glad to relocate to Waterloo for this role. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience in real-time and edge ML can support \clTeam{} at onsemi. My portfolio, publications and open datasets are at \href{\clSite}{nishal.xyz}.
